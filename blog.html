DAILY BLOG

Thinking out loud.
One insight at a time.

Some people journal. I write about what I learned today. It forces clarity — if I can't explain it simply, I didn't really learn it. These are the insights that stuck.

July 31, 2026

The Interface War Is Over. Voice Won.

ChatGPT's latest update lets you speak and it acts — in real time. Not "transcribe then process." Act while you're still talking. This is the death of the text box for most professional workflows. I've been dictating everything for weeks now, and going back to typing feels like going back to a flip phone. The keyboard isn't going away, but it's becoming what the pen became after the typewriter — a tool for specific moments, not the default. The default is now your voice. And the professionals who figure this out first will operate at 3x the speed of those still staring at blinking cursors.

July 30, 2026

I Cloned Myself. It Was Unsettling and Useful.

HeyGen lets you create a video avatar that looks like you, sounds like you, and delivers content you never recorded. I made one this week. The uncanny valley is mostly gone — my clone maintains eye contact, has natural pauses, and even has my micro-expressions down. The immediate use case: I can now produce video walkthroughs of my projects without blocking 2 hours to film, re-film, edit, and upload. The deeper implication: personal branding at scale becomes possible for people who don't have time to be content creators. You record your training data once. Your clone does the rest.

July 29, 2026

Loop Mode Changed My Relationship With AI

Discovered something called /loop in Claude Code this week. Instead of prompting → waiting → reviewing → prompting again, you set the AI on a recurring task and it self-paces. It checks, adjusts, iterates — while you do other things. This is the shift from "AI as a tool I use" to "AI as a colleague who works alongside me." The mental model change is significant. I'm no longer the bottleneck in the feedback loop. The AI is working even when I'm not actively directing it.

July 28, 2026

Dictation Makes You Think Differently

I've been dictating instead of typing for most of my work now. Here's what nobody tells you: it doesn't just make you faster — it makes you think differently. When you type, you edit as you go. You second-guess mid-sentence. When you dictate, you're forced to think in complete thoughts before speaking them. It's closer to how you'd explain something to a colleague. The output is more natural, more conversational, and ironically more clear. My prompts got better. My emails got better. My writing got better. All because I stopped typing.

July 27, 2026

Your Digital Clone Is Your Leverage

Here's how I'm thinking about HeyGen and tools like it: every professional has a limited number of hours. You can either spend those hours doing things only you can do, or you can spend them on repeatable communication tasks — recording walkthroughs, explaining processes, introducing yourself. A video clone handles the second category entirely. The math is simple: if recording and editing a 3-minute video takes 45 minutes, and your clone generates it in 2 minutes from a script, you just bought back 43 minutes per video. Multiply that across a career of content creation. The leverage is absurd.

I used to think the AI advantage was about quality — better analysis, fewer errors. I was wrong. The real advantage is speed. When you can dictate a complex prompt in 30 seconds instead of typing it in 3 minutes, you iterate 6x more in the same hour. When your AI loops autonomously while you're in a meeting, you're producing output during time that used to be dead. The professionals who move fastest with these tools aren't just doing better work — they're doing more experiments, testing more approaches, and compounding their learning faster than everyone else.

July 25, 2026

Voice-First AI Is the Real Copilot

ChatGPT's voice mode can now take actions while you speak to it. Think about that. You're not dictating a prompt and waiting. You're having a conversation and things are happening in real time. This is what "copilot" was always supposed to mean. Not a chatbot you tab over to. An assistant that's actively working while you're actively thinking. The gap between thought and execution just collapsed. For finance professionals drowning in manual work, this isn't a nice-to-have feature. It's the most important interface shift since the spreadsheet.

July 23, 2026

Autonomous Loops Are How Agents Should Work

Most people use AI like a vending machine — put in a request, get a result, evaluate, repeat. The /loop pattern flips this. You define what "done" looks like, set constraints, and let the agent iterate until it gets there. No babysitting. No micro-prompting. This is how I want all my AI tools to work eventually: I describe the outcome, set the guardrails, and walk away. Come back to a finished product, not a half-baked first draft that needs 4 more rounds of feedback. We're not there yet for everything, but for code and writing tasks, we're close.

July 22, 2026

The Typing Tax

I timed myself this week. A complex prompt that takes me 3 minutes to type takes 40 seconds to dictate. That's a 4.5x speed difference on input alone. Across a full day of AI-assisted work — 30+ prompts — that's over an hour saved just on the input side. And the quality is better because dictation captures my actual thinking cadence, not the edited-while-typing version. I'm calling it the "typing tax" — the invisible cost you pay every time you default to a keyboard when your voice would be faster and more natural.
